**Investigating the Multimodal Nature of Sound Symbolism in Online Language Use: A Large-Scale Analysis of Phonological Correspondences across 17 Languages**

**Introduction**

Sound symbolism is a fundamental concept in Linguistics that refers to the phenomenon where sound patterns convey meaning beyond their phonetic properties. Despite its significance, the study of sound symbolism remains largely focused on individual sounds or phonemes, neglecting potential multimodal interactions between different sound components. Recent developments in computational models and machine learning techniques have made it possible to analyze vast amounts of linguistic data, providing an unprecedented opportunity to explore sound symbolic patterns at a large scale. This research aims to investigate the multimodal nature of sound symbolism in online language use by examining phonological correspondences across 17 languages.

**Objectives**

This study has three main objectives:

1. **Investigate phonological correspondences**: Analyze the frequency and distribution of sound-semantic mappings (e.g., /k/ with concepts like "hardness" or "sharpness") across 17 languages.
2. **Examine multimodal interactions**: Identify potential relationships between different sound components (phonemes, syllables, stress patterns) that convey distinct meanings in each language.
3. **Compare cross-linguistic patterns**: Compare the frequency and similarity of phonological correspondences across languages to better understand their shared cognitive biases.

**Methodology**

This study employs a large-scale analysis approach using publicly available online corpora:

1. **Data Selection:** Utilize publicly available online corpora, including Wortschatz.net (German, English, French), WordNet (English), and Multilingual Wikipedia (17 languages).
2. **Text Preprocessing:** Apply tokenization, stopword removal, and stemming to prepare the text for analysis.
3. **Phonological Analysis:** Use the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (CMUPD) to transcribe words into phonemes and apply phonetic algorithms to extract relevant sound features.
4. **Correspondence Mining:** Employ frequent pattern mining techniques to identify sound-semantic mappings in each language.
5. **Cross-Linguistic Comparison:** Compute similarity measures between languages using their sound correspondences.

**Expected Outcomes**

This research is expected to:

1. Provide a comprehensive understanding of the multimodal nature of sound symbolism in online language use.
2. Shed light on shared cognitive biases and cultural influences on linguistic expression by comparing phonological correspondences across languages.
3. Demonstrate the effectiveness of computational models and machine learning techniques for analyzing large-scale linguistic data.

**Potential Impact**

This research has the potential to make significant contributions to the field of Linguistics, particularly in the area of Sound Symbolism:

1. **New insights into sound symbolism**: This study will provide a comprehensive understanding of the multimodal nature of sound symbolism.
2. **Comparative analysis**: The comparison of phonological correspondences across languages will shed light on shared cognitive biases and cultural influences on linguistic expression.
3. **Methodological contributions**: This research will demonstrate the effectiveness of computational models and machine learning techniques for analyzing large-scale linguistic data.
