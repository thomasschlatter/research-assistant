**Introduction**

Sound symbolism is a fundamental aspect of human language, where sounds are associated with meanings and concepts. Despite decades of research on sound symbolism, the relationship between acoustic properties and symbolic meanings remains poorly understood. Recent studies have highlighted the importance of auditory features, such as pitch, loudness, and spectral characteristics, in shaping phonological and lexical representations. This study aims to investigate the role of auditory features in sound symbolic effects across languages and linguistic domains.

**Objectives**

This study has three primary objectives:

1. To investigate the relationship between acoustic properties (e.g., pitch, loudness, spectral characteristics) and sound symbolic effects in a large-scale corpus.
2. To explore whether these relationships hold across different languages and linguistic domains (e.g., phonology, morphology, syntax).
3. To identify patterns of variation and consistency in sound symbolic associations that are driven by auditory features.

1. **Dataset:** The JSTOR Corpus (2023) will be used as the primary dataset for this study.

4. **Corpus processing:** The processed data will be analyzed using statistical methods (e.g., regression analysis, clustering algorithms) to identify patterns of association between auditory features and sound symbolic effects.

The expected outcomes of this study are:
