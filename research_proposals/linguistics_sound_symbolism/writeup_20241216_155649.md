**Investigating the Phonological Basis of Emotion Expression in Sound Symbolic Languages: A Comparative Analysis of 13 Publicly Available Datasets**

**Introduction**

Despite significant advances in Sound Symbolism research, a comprehensive understanding regarding how phonological properties of languages relate to emotional expression remains elusive. This knowledge gap hinders our ability to predict and explain language-specific emotional nuances across different cultures. The present study aims to fill this gap by examining 13 publicly available datasets from various sound symbolic language (SSL) families.

**Objectives**

This research has three primary objectives:

1. **Systematic Analysis of Sound Symbolic Properties**: To systematically analyze the sound symbolic properties (e.g., phonetic similarity, frequency, and distribution) of words expressing emotions across the selected datasets.
2. **Comparative Phonological Analysis**: To compare the phonological characteristics of emotion-related words in different SSLs to identify commonalities and divergences.
3. **Linguistic Typology and Emotional Expression**: To examine the impact of linguistic typology on emotional expression by investigating how language-specific features (e.g., tone, click consonants, whistling) influence emotional nuances.

**Methodology**

This study will utilize 13 publicly available datasets from SSL families, including tonal languages, click languages, and whistled languages. The methodology involves the following steps:

1. **Dataset Selection**: This study will rely exclusively on publicly available datasets and published corpora.
2. **Data Analysis**: Using publicly available software tools (e.g., Praat and Audacity), we will extract phonetic features from emotion-related words in each dataset and apply statistical methods to compare the phonological properties across datasets.
3. **Comparative Analysis**: We will use multivariate statistics to examine how linguistic typology influences emotional expression by comparing the results across SSL families.

**Expected Outcomes**

The expected outcomes of this study are:

1. **Systematic Understanding of Sound Symbolic Properties**: A comprehensive understanding of the sound symbolic properties of words expressing emotions in various SSLs.
2. **Phonological Comparison Across SSL Families**: A comparative analysis of phonological characteristics of emotion-related words across different SSLs, highlighting commonalities and divergences.
3. **Linguistic Typology and Emotional Expression**: An examination of how linguistic typology influences emotional expression, providing insights into language-specific features that shape emotional nuances.

**Potential Impact**

This study has the potential to significantly contribute to our understanding of Sound Symbolism, particularly in relation to phonological properties and emotional expression. The findings of this research will be relevant not only to linguists but also to cognitive scientists, psychologists, and philosophers interested in emotion representation and language. By leveraging publicly available datasets and published corpora, this study ensures that no human subject research is required, adhering to the highest standards of academic integrity.
