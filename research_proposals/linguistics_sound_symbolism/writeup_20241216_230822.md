**Introduction**

Sound Symbolism is a long-standing concept in Linguistics that proposes a connection between sound and meaning. Recent advances in computational models of word meaning and lexical semantics have led to renewed interest in this area. However, there remains a significant gap in understanding the relationship between orthographic, phonological, and semantic properties in words exhibiting Sound Symbolic effects. This study aims to fill this gap by investigating the interplay between these properties across languages, using existing linguistic resources.

**Objectives**

This research has three primary objectives:

1. **Investigate distribution of specific phonological features**: To analyze how certain phonological features (e.g., nasals, liquids) are distributed across VerbNet's classes, using OMW's comprehensive coverage of languages.
2. **Examine relationships between orthography and semantics**: To investigate how orthographic patterns (e.g., syllable structure, stress placement) relate to semantic meanings within these verb classes.
3. **Explore variability of Sound Symbolic effects**: To explore the degree to which Sound Symbolic effects manifest differently across various languages, accounting for linguistic typology and cultural context.

**Methodology**

This study will employ a mixed-methods approach:

1. **Corpus analysis**: OMW's vast dataset (~40 million entries) will be used to gather phonological, morphological, and semantic data for each verb class.
2. **Phonetic-orthographic matching**: An existing computational tool (e.g., the OpenPhonology dictionary) will be leveraged to align orthographic patterns with phonological features across languages.
3. **Multivariate analysis**: Statistical models will be applied to identify relationships between orthography, phonology, and semantics within verb classes.

**Expected Outcomes**

This research has the potential to:

1. Shed light on the intricate connections between sound, meaning, and form in language.
2. Inform theories of Sound Symbolism and its applications in linguistic typology, lexical semantics, and language acquisition.
3. Provide novel insights into the variability of Sound Symbolic effects across languages and cultures.

**Potential Impact**

The findings of this study have significant potential to:

1. **Advance our understanding of language**: By revealing the complex relationships between sound, meaning, and form, this research can contribute to a deeper comprehension of linguistic structures.
2. **Inform applications in language learning and teaching**: The insights gained from this study can be applied in educational settings, helping learners to develop a more nuanced understanding of language.
3. **Influence future research directions**: This study's findings can guide the development of new research agendas, exploring the intersection of sound, meaning, and form in language.

**Public Dataset and No Human Subjects Research Required**

The research will rely exclusively on the Open Multilingual Wordnet (OMW) dataset, which is publicly available through the Stanford Natural Language Processing Group's repository. This study involves no human subjects or new data collection, ensuring compliance with all relevant regulations and guidelines.
