**Title:** Investigating the Phonological Basis of Affective Symbolism in Emotive Consonant Clusters: A Corpus-Based Analysis Using the JSTOR Database and the Open-Source CLDF Framework.

**Introduction**

Sound Symbolism is a well-studied phenomenon in Linguistics, where humans associate certain sound patterns with specific meanings or emotions. Despite significant research efforts, the phonological basis of affective symbolism – i.e., how sounds contribute to emotional connotations – remains poorly understood, particularly in relation to consonant clusters (i.e., sequences of two or more consonants). This study aims to address this knowledge gap by analyzing the distribution and semantic associations of emotive consonant clusters in a large corpus.

Recent research has highlighted the importance of corpus-based approaches for investigating Sound Symbolism ([1], [2]) and the development of open-source frameworks like CLDF (Cognitive Linguistics Data Format) ([3]) that have streamlined data processing and analysis across various linguistic domains. This study leverages these advancements to investigate emotive consonant clusters in a large corpus of texts from diverse languages.

**Objectives**

This research has three primary objectives:

1. **Identify emotive consonant clusters**: Extract and categorize consonant clusters that are commonly associated with emotions or affective connotations.
2. **Investigate phonological properties**: Analyze the phonological properties (e.g., place, manner, voicing) of emotive consonant clusters to understand their contribution to emotional connotations.
3. **Examine distribution and semantic associations**: Investigate the distribution and semantic associations of emotive consonant clusters across different linguistic contexts.

**Methodology**

This study employs a corpus-based approach using publicly available text corpora from JSTOR (Journal Storage database) and the open-source CLDF framework to annotate and extract emotive consonant clusters. The methodology involves four stages:

1. **Corpus selection**: Utilize publicly available text corpora from JSTOR, which contains over 8,000 journals in various disciplines.
2. **Data processing**: Apply the CLDF framework to annotate and extract emotive consonant clusters from the corpus.
3. **Phonological analysis**: Use phonetic software (e.g., PCTOOL) to identify and analyze the phonological properties of extracted consonant clusters.
4. **Semantic association analysis**: Employ statistical techniques (e.g., topic modeling, sentiment analysis) to examine the semantic associations of emotive consonant clusters.

**Expected Outcomes**

This study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the phonological basis of affective symbolism in emotive consonant clusters. The expected outcomes are:

* A detailed categorization of emotive consonant clusters and their corresponding phonological properties.
* Insights into the distribution and semantic associations of emotive consonant clusters across different linguistic contexts.
* A deeper comprehension of how sounds contribute to emotional connotations, particularly in relation to consonant clusters.

**Potential Impact**

This study has the potential to significantly contribute to our understanding of Sound Symbolism, particularly in relation to emotive consonant clusters. By investigating the phonological basis of affective symbolism, this research aims to shed new light on the intricate relationships between sound and emotion, ultimately enriching our comprehension of human language and cognition.
