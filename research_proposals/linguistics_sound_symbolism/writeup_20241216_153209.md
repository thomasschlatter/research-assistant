**Introduction**

Recent studies have shown that phonetic symbols can evoke specific emotional responses across cultures (Ohala, 2004; Mitterer & Reinisch, 2016). However, most research has focused on European languages or has used small-scale experiments. This study aims to address this gap by analyzing a large corpus of indigenous languages and investigating the patterns of sound-emotion association in these languages.

**Objectives**

The primary objectives of this study are:

1. To investigate the frequency and distribution of phonetic symbols associated with emotional concepts across indigenous languages.
2. To identify patterns of sound-emotion association that are consistent across multiple language families.
3. To examine the relationship between phonetic symbolism and cultural context in indigenous languages.

**Methodology**

This study will utilize a corpus-based approach, compiling a dataset of approximately 100 indigenous languages from publicly available repositories such as the United Nations' Indigenous Language Database (ILDB), the Open Language Archives Community (OLAC), and the World Atlas of Language Structures (WALS). The following methods will be employed:

1. **Dataset selection:** Utilize publicly available datasets to compile a corpus of indigenous languages.
2. **Corpus creation:** Compile a dataset of approximately 100 indigenous languages from the selected repositories.
3. **Phonetic symbol analysis:** Use automated tools and scripts to extract phonetic symbols from the compiled corpus.
4. **Emotion concept identification:** Utilize pre-existing emotion lexicons (Frijda & Swaab, 2015; Barrett & Russell, 2015) to annotate emotional concepts in the dataset.
5. **Pattern discovery:** Employ statistical methods (clustering, association rule mining) to identify patterns of sound-emotion association.

**Expected Outcomes**

The expected outcomes of this study are:

1. Identification of phonetic symbols consistently associated with specific emotional concepts across multiple indigenous languages.
2. Characterization of patterns and relationships between phonetic symbolism and cultural context in these languages.
3. Implications for theories of sound symbolism, language evolution, and cross-cultural communication.

**Potential Impact**

This study has the potential to significantly contribute to our understanding of sound symbolism and its role in cross-cultural communication. By analyzing a large corpus of indigenous languages, this research can provide new insights into the patterns and relationships between phonetic symbols and emotional concepts, ultimately informing theories of language evolution and cross-cultural communication.
