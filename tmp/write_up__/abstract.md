The exploration of sound symbolism, where phonetic sounds convey meaning beyond arbitrary linguistic conventions, is a profound dimension of linguistic research. Despite its significance, the cross-linguistic understanding of sound symbolism, especially in ideophones, remains a largely untapped area. This paper presents a computational approach to investigating sound symbolism in ideophones across multiple languages, utilizing publicly available datasets. By employing advanced machine learning techniques such as random forests, as demonstrated in the study by Kilpatrick et al. (2023), we aim to discern the patterns and principles of sound symbolism that are universally shared across languages. This research builds on the innovative methodologies developed in recent studies, such as the use of vision-and-language models to probe sound symbolism in artificial intelligence settings (Alper and Averbuch-Elor, 2023). The datasets analyzed include iconicity ratings of Japanese ideophones, Russian onomatopoeias, Haitian Creole ideophones, and English ideophones, allowing for a comprehensive cross-linguistic analysis. Our methodology involves data preparation, feature extraction, and model training to predict sound-symbolic associations, drawing insights from computational advancements in fields such as explainable artificial intelligence (Adadi and Berrada, 2018). The expected contributions of this study include empirical evidence supporting cross-linguistic sound symbolism, development of predictive models, and enhanced understanding of phonosemantic relationships. By leveraging existing datasets and computational techniques, this research aims to address significant gaps in the literature, offering valuable insights into universal linguistic phenomena and the cognitive underpinnings of language.
