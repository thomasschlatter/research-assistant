**Research Paper Proposal**

**Title:** "Exploring Cross-Linguistic Sound Symbolism in Ideophones: A Computational Approach Using Publicly Available Datasets"

**Abstract:**
The phenomenon of sound symbolism—where sounds convey meaning beyond arbitrary language conventions—remains a critical area of linguistics with many unexplored dimensions. This research aims to address a gap in understanding cross-linguistic sound symbolism by investigating ideophones across multiple languages using computational methods. By leveraging existing datasets, this study will explore the patterns and principles of sound symbolism that are shared across languages, contributing to a deeper understanding of universal linguistic phenomena.

**1. Introduction**
Sound symbolism is a linguistic phenomenon where there is a non-arbitrary association between phonetic sounds and meaning. Despite its significance, the understanding of cross-linguistic sound symbolism, particularly in ideophones, remains limited. This study aims to analyze the sound-symbolic patterns found in ideophones across different languages using publicly available datasets, thereby expanding the understanding of this universal linguistic feature.

**2. Recent Developments**
Recent studies, such as those utilizing vision-and-language models, have demonstrated the potential to explore sound symbolism computationally. The work by Alper and Averbuch-Elor (2023) on sound symbolism in AI models and Kilpatrick et al.'s (2023) research on sound symbolism in Pokémon names highlight innovative methods for computational analysis of sound-meaning relationships.

**3. Research Objectives**
- To identify and analyze sound-symbolic patterns in ideophones across multiple languages.
- To assess the degree of cross-linguistic similarity in sound symbolism.
- To develop computational models that can predict sound-symbolic associations in ideophones.

**4. Methodology**
**Datasets:**
- **ICL: Iconicity ratings for 391 Japanese ideophones** ([icon_japanese.csv](https://osf.io/download/8hfk9/))
- **Sound symbolic potential of Russian onomatopoeias** (Repository ID: mjb4z)
- **Haitian Creole Ideophones: Typology of a Word Class** (Repository ID: qesb7)
- **At-issueness of Ideophones in English** ([data_USEideo_annotated.csv](https://osf.io/download/yd4g6/))

**Tools:**
- **Python libraries**: pandas, scipy, sklearn, and seaborn for data manipulation and analysis.
- **Machine Learning**: Random forests for classification tasks, inspired by Kilpatrick et al. (2023).

**Procedure:**
1. **Data Preparation**: Extract and preprocess ideophone data from the specified datasets to ensure consistency in format and features.
2. **Feature Extraction**: Identify phonetic and semantic features of ideophones that may correlate with sound symbolism.
3. **Model Training**: Use machine learning algorithms (e.g., random forests) to train models on sound-symbolic associations within and across languages.
4. **Cross-Linguistic Analysis**: Compare model outputs to identify patterns of sound symbolism that are universal versus language-specific.

**5. Expected Contributions**
- Provide empirical evidence for cross-linguistic sound symbolism in ideophones using computational methods.
- Develop models capable of predicting sound-symbolic associations, enhancing understanding of phonosemantic relationships.
- Contribute to linguistic theory by offering insights into universal principles of sound symbolism.

**6. Conclusion**
By leveraging existing datasets and advanced computational techniques, this study seeks to uncover the intricacies of sound symbolism in ideophones across languages, thereby closing a significant research gap in linguistics. The outcomes are expected to have broad implications for our understanding of language universals and the cognitive basis of language.

**Compliance with Ethical Standards**
This study exclusively utilizes publicly available datasets and published corpora, ensuring no human subject research or new data collection is involved.
